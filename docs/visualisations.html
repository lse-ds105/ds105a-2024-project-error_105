In this section, we explore the visualizations we created during the process of mapping Pokémon to biomes. These visualizations illustrate key findings from our analysis, as well as how we developed the interactive map.

Effect of Latitude and Longitude on Temperature

We analyzed the impact of geographical coordinates (latitude and longitude) on temperature. The three graphs below demonstrate this relationship:

Graph 1: Latitude vs Temperature

Graph 2: Longitude vs Temperature

Graph 3: Combined Latitude and Longitude vs Temperature

Our analysis pointed to latitude being the primary factor in determining temperature. This finding is consistent with geography literature that supports the idea that temperature decreases as we move away from the equator (Springer, 2011).

Regression Analysis: Latitude as a Primary Determinant of Temperature

We ran two regressions that further supported our conclusion. Both analyses indicated that latitude was the most significant predictor of temperature, which aligns with existing geographic theory.

  • Regression 1: Latitudes explained 80% of the variance in temperature.
  • Regression 2: Longitude had a negligible effect on temperature.

The findings were consistent with the expectations from geography, where higher latitudes correspond to cooler temperatures.

The Final Map

The final map displays Pokémon in real-world biomes and is fully interactive. Some of the map's key features include:

  • Stacked Pokémon: Some Pokémon may appear stacked in the same location due to overlapping biomes or identical ranking. This issue can be resolved through layering or by adjusting their visibility in the legend.
  • Search Bar: Users can search for specific Pokémon to see where they are located globally.
  • Map Updates: The map updates daily, allowing for real-time changes based on weather forecasts and Pokémon migrations.

Our team members @Hailey and @Adrian are working on improving these features and addressing any remaining issues related to map interaction and visualization.
